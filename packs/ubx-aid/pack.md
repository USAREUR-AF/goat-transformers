## The message

`wire bits`, `bit_order lsb`, `word_bytes 1`; hex text. One UBX frame without
its checksum; numbers are little-endian:

| Bytes | Content | In the pack |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | sync characters 0xB5 0x62 | common `when` |
| 2 | class, 0x0B | common `when` |
| 3 | id | per-kind `when` |
| 4-5 | payload length in bytes | per-kind `when` = the definition's fixed length |
| 6 … | payload | the kind's `field` lines, at their offsets |

The message is the frame without its checksum, in both directions: decode takes
the sync characters, header and payload, and encode writes them. A framer adds
the two checksum bytes before sending and checks them on receipt: an 8-bit
Fletcher checksum over class, id, length and payload (a running sum A of the
bytes and a running sum B of A, each modulo 256), written after the payload.
That is the item "Layered wires and framing" of `docs/roadmap.md`. A message
whose identity includes the first payload byte (the MGA messages) has a per-kind
`when` on that byte. Where the source chooses among definitions of one identity
by the value of one payload byte (`ubxvariants.py`: a version or type byte), the
kind it chooses that way has a `when` on that byte; the other definitions of the
identity are told apart by their length.
